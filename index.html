How to film a package unboxing

One continuous video — sealed box to empty box. Here's how to create a clear record of what arrived and what condition it was in, so any problem is easy to prove and resolve.

🎉

They're here!

That rush when the package finally lands on your doorstep — enjoy it! But before you tear into that box, hit record. A few minutes of filming now protects everything you just spent weeks waiting for. Here's exactly how to do it right.

📋 Educational reference only. This page teaches general documentation practices for receiving parcels. It is not legal advice and is not affiliated with any seller or carrier.
01

Why film it

Thirty seconds of setup can save you weeks of arguing later.

A proper unboxing video is a continuous record of four things: what arrived, how the package was sealed, what was inside, and the condition of everything the moment you received it.

That record becomes invaluable the moment anything is missing, incorrect, damaged, or leaking. And it establishes a timeline — sealed package → opening → contents revealed → items removed → empty box — that lets someone who wasn't there understand exactly what happened.

Without that sequence, a missing item turns into a guessing game: never packed? Wrong quantity? Opened in transit? Removed after delivery? The video doesn't answer everything by itself, but it gives any investigation a solid starting point.
02

Before you start

Get everything ready before the box is open — there's no pause button on evidence.

  • Charged device with free storage — a dead battery mid-unboxing ruins the whole point.
  • Good lighting — labels, seals, and damage must be readable on camera.
  • A clean, stable surface with room to lay contents out individually.
  • Frame the package and your hands — both need to stay visible together.
  • Film somewhere private — avoid spots where other people might wander into the recording.
⛔ The golden rule: never start recording after the package is already open. The most valuable evidence is a continuous take that begins while the box is still sealed.
03

The opening

Steps 1–4: sealed proof, steady frame, careful opening.

1

Record before you cut

Hit record before any cuts or tears. Show the whole box first: the outside, shipping labels, tracking info, tape and seals — plus any damage (dents, holes, tears, crushed corners, wet areas). Linger on damage from several angles.

2

Prove it's still sealed

Demonstrate the package hasn't been opened: sealed tape, security seals, shrink wrap, tamper-evident packing. Rotate slowly so every side gets its moment on camera.

3

Keep it in frame

From here on, the box stays on camera. Don't move it off-screen, cover it for long stretches, walk away mid-recording, pull items out of view, or switch to a second recording halfway through.

4

Open normally, don't rush

Show the inside of the box, padding, documents, and contents as first seen. If anything looks damaged, leaking, or wrong — show its condition first, before touching or rearranging it.

04

Item by item

Steps 5–6: slow down — this is where the details live.

📦 One at a time

Remove each item individually and hold it close enough that its identifying details read clearly on camera:

  • Product name and description
  • Labels, quantities, lot or batch numbers
  • Expiration dates and packaging condition

Give the camera time to focus. Small text? Bring the item closer or adjust the camera — don't just wave it past the lens.

🔢 Verify quantities

Got several identical items? Group them together in frame so the count is undeniable — and count out loud while recording: "one, two, three, four."

⛔ Count looks off? Don't stop recording. Finish the entire unboxing so the complete contents are documented, then deal with the discrepancy.
05

Damage & the finish

Steps 7–9: document problems, then prove the box is empty.

🔍 Document every problem

Crushed packaging, broken containers, tears, leaks, missing labels, broken seals, wrong items, wrong quantities, moisture damage — show each one from several angles and take your time.

Keep the damaged item with its original packaging instead of throwing anything away immediately.

🏁 Don't stop early

Found one problem? Keep going. There could be a second issue hiding behind the first — a missing item and a damaged one, for example. Only a complete video proves the full story.

📭 Show the empty box

Once everything is out, film the empty interior — move packing material, inserts, and bags aside so it's obvious nothing is hiding underneath. The goal: establish on camera that the package was completely emptied during the recording.

06

Keep the original

Step 10: your footage is only evidence if it still exists.

Don't delete or edit the original recording. Screenshots and trimmed clips are handy for sharing, but they're no substitute for the complete, unedited file.

Keep everything together somewhere safe:

  • The original full-length video
  • Photos of damage, labels, and individual items
  • Shipping information and tracking details
  • Your order confirmation or invoice
  • Any correspondence about the issue
Edited versions are fine for sending to a seller — just never let the edited copy become the only copy.
07

Reporting an issue

If something's wrong, report it fast — and bring receipts.

Contact the seller or representative as soon as reasonably possible. Keep the message factual and concise, and include:

  • What you received
  • What appears to be wrong
  • When the package arrived
  • That you filmed the complete unboxing
  • The photos and video
  • Any order or shipment details they need

Damage? Describe it precisely. Wrong item? State clearly what you expected versus what actually arrived.

"Hi — my package arrived today and one expected item seems to be missing. I filmed the complete unboxing from the sealed box to the empty box, and I've attached the video and photos. Let me know what else you need to look into this."
08

Mistakes to avoid

Eight ways people accidentally ruin perfectly good evidence.

❌

Starting after opening

The box's original sealed condition is gone forever.

❌

Stopping halfway

Any gap in recording is a gap anyone can question.

❌

Going off-camera

What the lens never saw might as well never have happened.

❌

Filming too far away

Labels and quantities become unreadable mush.

❌

Moving too fast

Blurry fly-bys capture nothing usable.

❌

Ditching packaging early

Boxes and padding can hold proof of damage or tampering.

❌

Photos only

Still images can't show the continuous timeline a video does.

❌

Editing the only copy

Always keep the raw original alongside any trimmed version.

09

Master checklist

Run through this every time. Future-you says thanks.

🎬 Before opening

  • Start recording
  • Show the entire package
  • Show shipping information
  • Show seals and tape
  • Document visible damage
  • Camera stable and focused

📦 During the unboxing

  • Keep the package in frame
  • Open the package on camera
  • Show the inside packaging
  • Remove items individually
  • Show labels clearly
  • Verify quantities
  • Document damage or abnormalities
  • Don't pause or stop recording

🏁 At the end

  • Remove all contents
  • Show the empty package
  • Show all items together
  • Take extra photos if needed
  • Save the original video
  • Keep the original packaging
  • Report problems promptly
